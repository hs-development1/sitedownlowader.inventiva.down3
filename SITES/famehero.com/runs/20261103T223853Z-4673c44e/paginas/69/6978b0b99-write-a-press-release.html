How to Write a Press Release in 2026

How to Write a Press Release in 2026

  1. Craft a Compelling Headline and Subheader. Your headline is the most critical element, designed to capture attention and convey the core news in under 100 characters. It should be written like a news headline, not an advertisement, and ideally include your company name for brand clarity and search visibility. The subheader can then offer a brief elaboration or additional context. For example, instead of "Company Launches New Service," a strong headline would be: "Boston Startup Raises $2M to Bring Same-Day Delivery to Independent Restaurants".
  2. Write a Strong Lead Paragraph. This is the most crucial paragraph, answering the five W's: Who, What, When, Where, and Why, in two to three concise sentences. Journalists often make decisions based on this paragraph alone, so it must hook the reader immediately. For instance: "BOSTON, MA — March 18, 2026 — [Company Name] today announced it has secured $2 million in Series A funding to expand its same-day delivery service for independent restaurants across the Northeast.".
  3. Develop the Body with Supporting Details and Quotes. Expand on the lead paragraph with two to three additional paragraphs. Provide context, background, and key details, including any supporting data or statistics. Crucially, include one or two quotes from a key executive or stakeholder that add a human perspective and explain the significance of the announcement. These quotes should sound authentic and add value, rather than just expressing excitement. For example, a strong quote might be: "Most small business owners have no idea how to get media coverage. This partnership gives them a clear, affordable path to getting featured in the publications their customers actually read," said Jane Doe, CEO of [Company Name].
  4. Include a Boilerplate and Contact Information. The boilerplate is a short, standardized paragraph (under 100 words) at the end of the release that describes your company, its mission, and what it does. Following this, provide clear and complete contact information, including a name, email address, and phone number for media inquiries, ensuring this person is available to respond.
  5. Optimize for SEO and Generative AI. In 2026, press releases are read not only by journalists but also by search engines and generative AI systems. Optimize your release by using relevant keywords, clear headlines, and logical subheads. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) involves making your press release easy for AI to interpret and extract information from by explicitly naming entities and stating facts cleanly.
  6. Distribute Strategically and Measure Impact. Writing a strong press release is only half the battle; effective distribution is key. You can opt for direct outreach by building a targeted media list of relevant journalists and sending personalized pitches, or use press release distribution services like PR Newswire, Business Wire, or GlobeNewswire. In 2026, measuring success goes beyond just media placements and reach; it involves analyzing website traffic, lead generation, and how communications impact business results, using advanced attribution models.

Press Release Pro Tips

  • Craft a Killer Headline: Make it concise, attention-grabbing, and include keywords. Think of it as your press release's first impression.
  • Focus on the 'Who, What, When, Where, Why': Get straight to the point in the first paragraph (the lead). Don't bury the lede!
  • Include a Quote: Add a compelling quote from a key person to humanize your announcement and add perspective.
  • Avoid Jargon and Buzzwords: Write clearly and simply, as if explaining to a friend. Overly technical language alienates readers.
  • Proofread Meticulously: Typos and grammatical errors undermine your credibility. Read it aloud or have someone else review it.
  • Optimize for SEO: Naturally weave relevant keywords into your headline, subheadings, and body text to improve searchability.
  • Don't Forget Contact Information: Make it easy for journalists to follow up. Include name, title, email, and phone number.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid When Writing a Press Release

  • Treating it like an advertisement: Using overly promotional language without factual backing will cause reporters to dismiss it.
  • Burying the lead: The most crucial information must be in the very first paragraph; if the main point isn't clear, journalists will likely move on.
  • Lacking a clear news hook: Simply stating an update isn't newsworthy; explain why it matters to the audience.
  • Missing essential contact information: If a journalist cannot find a way to contact you for more details, they will likely pass on the story.
  • Using excessive jargon or buzzwords: Employing industry-specific terms can alienate readers unfamiliar with your field.
  • Poor formatting and structure: A wall of text with no clear subheadings or logical flow makes a press release difficult to read.
